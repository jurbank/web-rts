# Blender RTS models

This skill makes rigid models of two kinds: **structures**, such as buildings, and **vehicles**, whose moving parts turn on pivots, such as turrets, barrels, wheels, and rotors. Models with a skeleton, such as infantry, animals, monsters, and walking mechs, use the `blender-rts-creatures` skill, and effects use the `rts-effects` skill. Pass `--kind vehicles` to the helper for a vehicle. Structures are the default.

Every model belongs to one set: a theme, such as `modern` or `cyberpunk`, which is the look of a map and its civic buildings, or a faction, such as `advanced-utopian`, which is the complete look of one side. A playable side's units belong to its faction. If the request doesn't name a set, ask. Read `docs/ART_DIRECTION.md` first for the rules every set follows, then the set's file, `docs/themes/<theme>.md` or `docs/factions/<faction>.md`, for its shape language, palette, surfaces, glow, signage, building types, and unit types. The set file decides the look, and a faction never borrows from a theme or another faction. A model that would fit another set has failed, even if it passes every check. If the set has no file yet, or its file has no row for the model's type, write it with the user before modeling. Read [the project pipeline](references/pipeline.md) for source and export paths, Blender conventions, and helper commands. Resolve paths from the web-rts workspace root. For an asset revision, open its existing source first and preserve its asset ID, anchor, part names, and intentional dimensions.

## Build the asset

1. Establish the model's type, world-space footprint, front, and moving parts. Take gameplay dimensions from the matching project definition or placement rules when available. A vehicle fits within its definition's `radius`. Record the radius in the source collection's `radius` property. When no definition exists, choose a provisional radius, note why in words on the collection, and describe the assumption when delivering.
2. Take the type's row from the set file: its signature silhouette, any accent, and required elements. Pick neighbors to line up beside it from the same set and kind, and match their scale and detail density, except where they break the set file. Line a vehicle up with one of its faction's buildings too, such as the factory that builds it, to check scale. A faction may add one theme building as a neighbor to check scale, but never matches its look. Give the model its own silhouette, and its own accent if the set uses accents, unless it is a designated variant. When the set has no approved models of that kind yet, build one at a time, and treat the look as provisional until the user approves it.
3. Block out the footprint, primary mass, and one or two features that identify the role from above, using the set's shape language. Export with `--blockout` and render it with `--with` the neighbors to check the silhouette, the top, and the front from the game camera before adding detail. Reuse matching modular geometry where it already exists.
4. Texture the asset with its set's atlas, as the pipeline describes. Read `art/blender/materials/<themes|factions>/<set>/atlas.json` to choose cards rather than viewing the atlas, and map faces onto cards with `scripts/atlas_cards.py` instead of writing UV code. Add a card when the set's atlas has nothing that fits the set file, then run `refresh` and export the set's models again, as the pipeline describes. Paint glow on the glow map where the set allows it, and paint a faction's finish on its surface map. Give a faction's building its team-colored element, in the form its faction file sets, with a material named `team`. Painted detail replaces modeled detail. Flat colors are for blockouts only, and for a vehicle's team material. Use Blender's own Python and numpy for any other image work instead of installing packages.
5. Save the editable model in its canonical source file. Put the render meshes for the default state in the named asset collection, with helpers and experiments in sibling collections. Mark the render collection as an Asset and give it a useful description. Keep procedural modeling modifiers editable in the source.
6. Export the asset without `--blockout`. The export joins a structure into one mesh per material. It fails when the GLB carries no image textures, no glow in a set that glows, more triangles than its kind's budget in the pipeline allows, no team material on a vehicle or a faction's building, or, for a vehicle, a part with an unusable name. When it names other exports of the set that embed an older atlas, export them again. Report the export's size, triangles and share of the budget, primitives, and textures, plus a vehicle's moving parts and sockets, and compare them with neighboring assets and the expected repetition on a typical map.
7. Render the export next to its closest neighbors, by day and by night for a faction, and have it reviewed as [the review guide](references/review.md) describes. Fix what the review finds, then export, render, and review again until the verdict is ship.
8. Rebuild the gallery whenever you add, remove, or rename an asset, after saving its source.
9. Deliver the review sheet and the export summary, and leave the GLB in staging. A ship verdict means the asset is ready for the user to look at. Copy it to `apps/website/public/models/<kind>/<themes|factions>/<set>/` only when the user approves it.

## Vehicles

A vehicle is seen from above, smaller than any building, turning in every direction, and often in a crowd. These rules add to the steps above.

- **The top says the role.** As a roof does for a building, the view from above tells a tank from a harvester or an artillery piece. Exaggerate what does: an oversized turret, a long barrel, a wide scoop. Keep the masses chunky so the silhouette survives at phone size.
- **The team color shows.** Map the faces that take the player's color like any other piece, then give them a material named `team` with a white base color, and join them into the part they ride on. The game multiplies that material by the player's color, and the review renders show a sample blue. Make the team area large enough to read at phone size, usually a broad band or panel on the top.
- **Moving parts turn on named pivots.** The hull is the root mesh. Each part that moves on its own is a child mesh, named in lowercase kebab-case, such as `turret`, `barrel`, `wheel-front-left`, or `rotor`, with its origin on its axis: a turret turns around its local Z, and a barrel pitches and a wheel spins around its local X. Join static detail into the part it rides on, so each part exports as one primitive, plus the team material.
- **Sockets mark where things happen.** Put an empty named `muzzle` at the end of each barrel, numbered `muzzle-2` and up for more, and others such as `exhaust` where effects attach. Parent each to the part it moves with.
- **Aircraft rest on the ground.** Author an aircraft as for any vehicle, with its lowest point at Z=0. The game sets its height.

Keep terrain, selection overlays, gameplay collision or navigation, and the motion of moving parts in their existing project systems. Use the Blender connector for an existing scene when helpful, or Blender's background CLI with `bpy` for repeatable modeling, export, rendering, and verification.

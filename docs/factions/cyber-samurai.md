# Cyber samurai

## Identity

A warrior clan builds fortified logistics hubs, refineries, and forges from stepped armor, dark optical glass, and lacquered steel. Castle architecture sets the proportions. Broad foundations and inset tiers give way to tall armor faces, exposed heat exchangers, and recessed optical instruments. Samurai influence shows in the battered bases, the cut of a shoulder or eave, the two-beam gates, and the broken-ring blade crest. Each structure has an exposed system large enough to identify its job from the game camera.

## Shape language

- **Stepped armor.** Use broad chamfered shoulders, inward-sloping faces, overhanging armor lips, and narrow recesses between tiers. Two or three substantial masses read at game scale. Keep tall silhouettes squat enough to leave nearby units visible.
- **Samurai profiles.** Use a clipped, subtly upswept brow, a low hipped canopy, or the silhouette of a two-post gate. Treat roofs as armor and working surfaces. Rolled steel tiles belong on smaller canopies and sheltered accents.
- **Visible machinery.** Expose one system that explains the building's role from above, such as a loading bridge, a round separator, or a long bank of heat exchangers. Give it a clear recess or frame. Paint the fine detail into the atlas.
- **Fortified base.** Use a low battered granite or composite plinth with inset tracks and service courts. Towers can use a taller base. Fronts face the apron, with a lacquered gate or a two-beam lintel built into a large entrance.
- **Blade masts.** A command or research structure can carry one compact blade-shaped mast. Keep projections thick enough to read from the game camera.

## Materials

Satin blue-black alloy and steel armor plates, dark optical glass, black lacquered framing, vermilion gate fittings, and a few brass or ceramic service parts. Use broad panel breaks, recessed status windows, segmented plating, and occasional laced-armor texture. Granite and gravel belong at ground level. Metal and lacquer are clean, with fine tool marks and deliberate bright edges. Stone may show age in its joints.

## Glow

- **Ice blue** `#8bc5de` identifies active optical systems, loading indicators, and separators. Use grouped windows behind dark frames and segmented rim lights.
- **Rose crimson** `#d96973` traces an inset armor seam or marks the blade of the crest. Keep the traces short and subordinate to the silhouette.
- **Amber** `#f2b866` identifies the forge's heat or a sheltered lantern.
- Keep emission to about a tenth of visible surfaces. Concentrate it in patterned recesses with enough dark steel around each strip to preserve the shape and team colors at night.

## Signage

The crest is an invented broken ring around an upright blade with a short guard. Use a white or brass ring and a crimson blade. Place it on gates, loading faces, or service paving. Service lanes, bay outlines, and indexing marks use off-white paint. Any incidental graphics use original geometric symbols.

## References

- **Stepped tower study.** Take the stacked black volumes, inset rose and blue instrument bands, and broad vertical armor channels. Compress the mass for the RTS camera and design original markings.
- **Clad pyramid study.** Take the inward-sloping armor faces, wide broken planes, and service gaps between overhanging tiers. Use the warm and icy lights assigned to each system in this brief.
- **Curved-eave and gate studies.** Take the heavy edge of a low roof, the swept tip of an armor brow, and the proportions of the vermilion entrance frame. Use these forms where they support the structure's function.

## Avoid

- Roof tiles across the full production silhouette, tall city towers, sharply curled eaves, or ornate replica castles.
- Full-face emissive cladding, rainbow lights, detached holograms, or signs competing with team colors.
- Unrelated roof props, heavy grime, exposed wire clutter, statues, and oversized swords.
- Real clan crests, real lettering used as ornament, and copied logos or facade patterns.
